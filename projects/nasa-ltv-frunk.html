Lunar Terrain Vehicle front trunk

NASA’s Lunar Terrain Vehicle ground test unit needed a front trunk whose hood opens itself on deployment. I picked up an existing bodywork concept and took it to a working assembly — gas springs, structure, mounts and a latch — inside a chassis that was already largely fixed.

Where it started

I joined the front-end effort partway through. An initial bodywork concept existed; what it did not have was a way to open, a way to stay open, or structure stiff enough to carry the loads that implies. The work was taking it from an exterior surface to an assembly that functions.

Sizing the gas springs

A hood that opens itself is a moment problem before it is a mechanism problem. I calculated the centre of mass of the hood assembly, then the gas-spring force and mounting geometry required for it to self-open on deployment. The force sets which spring you buy; the geometry — where the eyelets sit relative to the hinge line — decides whether that force is actually available through the whole travel rather than just at the start of it.

Structure and bodywork

I modified the top bodywork to integrate a hinge and designed three structural pieces to reinforce it, then refined the full sheet-metal exterior. Adding a hinge to a panel changes where its load goes: the reinforcement exists so the hood carries its own weight through the hinge instead of oil-canning around it.

Mounting hardware

The gas springs land on a billet mounting block with helicoil inserts — threads that survive being taken apart more than once, which matters on a test unit that will be reworked. The eyelet attachments are spaced with nylon spacers to reduce friction at the joint so the spring works against the hood rather than against its own mounting.

Latch and release

I designed the latch mounting and its sheet-metal bracketry to attach to the existing tube structure. Nothing new could be added to the frame, so the bracket had to reach the tubes that were already there and still put the latch where the hood closes.

The real constraint

Almost none of this was a blank sheet. I inherited CAD with existing conflicts, a frame I could not move and bodywork already drawn. Most of the design time went into packaging — finding the volume that a spring, a hinge and a latch could share without any of them interfering with each other or with the frame.

Handover

I took the design through reviews with the engineers on the programme and delivered the completed CAD assembly with technical documentation, so the work could carry on toward fabrication after my internship ended.
