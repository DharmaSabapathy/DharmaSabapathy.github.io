Quiet supersonic aircraft research

A NASA SEES research internship on commercial-scale quiet supersonic aircraft. The question was narrow and physical: which configuration changes actually reduce the sonic boom that reaches the ground?

The problem

Supersonic passenger flight over land is limited less by propulsion than by noise. The boom a body makes is set by how its cross-sectional area and lift are distributed along its length, which makes it a shaping problem — you are designing the pressure signature, and the aircraft is what is left over.

What I did

I worked on a national research team and led aircraft configuration development, building the geometry in CAD and evaluating it with CFD against sonic-boom reduction strategies. Each configuration change is only interesting if the solver says the near-field pressure signature moved in the right direction, so the loop was: change the geometry, run it, look at what the change actually bought.

Presenting it

I presented the research to NASA scientists and engineers, and at the AGU 2025 Annual Meeting.
