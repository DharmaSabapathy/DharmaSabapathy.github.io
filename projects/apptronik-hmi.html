Humanoid robot HMI handles

Handles an operator uses to physically manipulate a humanoid robot, designed from scratch. The shape was the easy part; the design problem was where the buttons go once real people start putting their hands on it.

The brief

Elevate Robotics needed a mechanical human-machine interface — a physical handle set for guiding and manipulating the robot directly. I worked with the engineering team to turn a loose need into high-level requirements before drawing anything, then owned the subsystem from concept through to the delivered part.

Ergonomics you cannot calculate

Handle geometry and button placement are not analytical problems. You make a version, give it to someone, and watch where their thumb actually lands. I went through several revisions on button positioning and ergonomics driven by beta-tester feedback, printing each iteration on FDM so the loop from “this feels wrong” to a new part in someone’s hands stayed short.

Why FDM for iteration and SLS for delivery

FDM is fast and cheap, which is what an ergonomics loop needs — the part only has to be the right shape. The final deliverable went to SLS, which gives isotropic strength and a surface that survives being handled, because by then the part had to work rather than just inform.

Designing for a process that does not exist yet

The prototypes were printed, but the production intent was injection moulding at scale. I carried DFM considerations for moulding through the CAD rather than leaving them to a later redesign — the point being that the geometry which survives prototyping should not have to be redrawn when it meets a tool.

Communicating it

I produced and presented technical documentation and the design iterations to the engineering team, and worked alongside engineers from other disciplines on design reviews, subsystem integration and troubleshooting.
