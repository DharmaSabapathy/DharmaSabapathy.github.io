What I work on

Four things every project comes back to

Whether it’s a rover hood or a ball transfer, the work is the same shape: figure out the force, make the geometry fit the space you actually have, and design it for how it will be built.

Mechanism design

Gas springs, latches, pivots and dead-axle rollers. Sizing the force first, then making the linkage fit inside the packaging that’s left.

Sheet metal & structure

Bodywork, brackets, billet mounts and reinforcement — designed around the bend, the fastener and the tube it has to land on.

System integration

Master sketches, space claims and interface control, so six subsystems designed by six people still assemble into one machine.

Prototype to production

FDM for iteration, SLS for the deliverable, and DFM decisions taken early enough that injection moulding stays possible at scale.

All projects

  • 2026 · NASA JSC

    Lunar Terrain Vehicle front trunk

    A self-opening hood for NASA’s LTV ground test unit. I calculated the centre of mass and the gas-spring force needed to lift it, then designed the sheet-metal structure, billet mounts and latch around a chassis I inherited.

    • SOLIDWORKS
    • Gas springs
    • Sheet metal

  • 2025 · Apptronik

    Humanoid robot HMI handles

    Control handles for physically manipulating a humanoid robot. Designed from scratch, revised against beta-tester feedback on button placement and ergonomics, delivered in SLS with injection moulding in mind.

    • Onshape
    • Ergonomics
    • DFM

  • 2026 · FRC 2468

    Competition robot integration

    Full-robot integration from the ground up. A master sketch defined every subsystem interface, space claim and the game-piece path through the machine before anyone detailed a part.

    • Master sketch
